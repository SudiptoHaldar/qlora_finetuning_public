## Summary

`python -m scripts.comparison_cost report` prices the ledger with `config/pricing.yaml` and
writes `reports/comparison/cost_model.json` and `.md`. It calls no AWS service.

- **Categories.** Each of the 86 effective ledger records is in one category. Which run is
  which comes from the six-arm record, the job record, the selection record, and the tuning
  record, never from an arm name alone.
- **Per arm.** The test pass, the cost per 1,000 questions, and per 1,000 correct answers.
  A Qwen arm has two figures: as run (the job's billable seconds) and steady (the run's own
  seconds, without the start-up).
- **Serving Qwen.** A batch job at 100 to 100,000 questions, and an always-on endpoint at
  100%, 50%, 10%, and 1% of the throughput the test job measured.
- **Break-evens.** Each Qwen arm against each Nova arm of its kind, as an always-on
  endpoint, as a batch job, and with the cost of making the model. Each is marked reachable
  or not against the instance's capacity and the account's request limits.
- **Against the bill.** `comparison_cost bill` appends an amount the owner read from the
  bill; the record puts it beside the model's total for the same days.

The owner's seven decisions were all taken as recommended. The hosting price read from the
public price list is $1.408 an hour for `ml.g5.xlarge`, the same as the training rate.
